# White logo-light.png blends hero and footer marks onto dark backgrounds

The hero and footer logos previously sat inside a white rounded card (`.logo-chip`) on the dark navy→teal gradient and navy footer — the "ugly white box" the owner rejected as not matching the page. This change ships a recolored white asset (`logo-light.png`) and places it directly on the dark surfaces with no wrapper box: `.logo-chip` is dropped from both DOM nodes, its CSS rule is deleted, the hero/footer `src` points at the white asset, and the stylesheet cache-bust is bumped `20260922k` → `20260922l`. The navbar colored logo is left untouched. The approach was chosen over a CSS `invert()` filter (fragile on a two-tone mark) and over keeping the chip (rejected by the owner).

Watch for: nothing blocking. The white asset is a verified clean silhouette, both dark surfaces render the mark legibly with no box, and the navbar is unchanged (all **confirmed** against code, the regenerated Pillow audit, and screenshots).

**Verdict**: APPROVED

## High-level view

The fix is a swap of asset plus removal of a wrapper, not a layout rework. The hero and footer `<img>` nodes drop the `logo-chip` class and point at `logo-light.png`; the dead `.logo-chip` rule (white background, radius, padding, shadow) is deleted so no renderable white box remains anywhere. Existing sizing/centering rules (`.hero-logo` 104px, `.footer-logo` 56px, `.footer-brand` centering) are retained and sit within the brief's ranges.

The new `logo-light.png` is a recolor of the source: every opaque pixel forced to pure `#FFFFFF`, original alpha copied unchanged. Because the source mark has zero semi-transparent pixels, the result is a hard-edged white silhouette with no gray fringe and no black box — re-verified independently here, not just taken from the commit message.

The navbar is genuinely untouched: it still references the colored `logo.png` via `.brand-logo` at 76px/60px, confirmed in both the HTML and the navbar screenshot. Bilingual `data-en`/`data-es` attributes, the language switcher, mobile nav, and aria-labels are all outside the diff. The logo `<img>` alt text changed from "IB Bridge logo" to "IB Bridge" — meaningful and appropriate for brand imagery.

## White asset generation and pixel integrity

`logo-light.png` was regenerated by recoloring opaque pixels to pure white and copying the source alpha unchanged. An independent Pillow re-check here (not relying on the commit message) confirms the output is RGBA 1536×1024 with bbox `(228, 144, 1307, 814)` — identical geometry to `logo.png` — with 211,709 opaque pixels, **0** non-white opaque pixels, and 1,361,155 fully-transparent pixels matching the source. The source's zero semi-transparent pixels guarantee a crisp silhouette with no gray halo, so the common failure modes (black box, leftover teal/navy color, aliased fringe) are all ruled out. **Confirmed.**

## Hero and footer blend onto the dark surfaces

Both DOM nodes dropped the `logo-chip` class and now reference `logo-light.png`. The `desktop-hero` and `desktop-footer` screenshots show the white mark sitting directly on the gradient and on the navy footer with no card, border, or shadow behind it, centered, and legible including the fine "Your bridge to IB success" sub-tagline inside the mark. The footer mark sits above the unchanged teal tagline. The `mobile-hero` screenshot shows the same boxless white logo centered on the gradient. The `.logo-chip` rule is deleted from `styles.css` and `grep` finds zero `logo-chip` references in either file — no renderable white box remains. **Confirmed.**

## Scope and accessibility boundary

The diff touches exactly `index.html` (two logo lines + cache-bust to `?v=20260922l`), `styles.css` (the single deleted `.logo-chip` rule), and adds `logo-light.png` — nothing stray in `script.js`, favicons, or `.nojekyll`. The navbar `.brand-logo` (colored `logo.png`, 76px/60px) and the bilingual `data-en`/`data-es` attributes, language switcher, mobile nav, and aria-labels all sit outside the diff, so none of the preserved-behavior requirements are at risk. The logo alt text moved from "IB Bridge logo" to "IB Bridge" — meaningful and dropping the redundant "logo" word for brand imagery.
